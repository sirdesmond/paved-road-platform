## Summary
One paragraph. What's changing and why it matters. A reader should be able to stop here and know the shape of it.

## Problem
The current state and its costs — in terms the org feels (latency, risk, toil, bus factor), not just technical dissatisfaction. Include the failure mode you must avoid, not just the one you're fixing.

## Goals
Bulleted, testable. If you can't measure it, it's not a goal.

## Non-goals
What this deliberately does not do. This section prevents scope arguments later and is usually the most-read part in review.

## Proposal
The design. Include the contract/interface first, then the flow. Diagrams where they earn their place.

## Alternatives considered
Each with an honest reason for rejection. Reviewers trust a proposal in proportion to how fairly it treats the alternatives — a strawman here undermines the whole document.

## Risks and mitigations
Table form. Include the "this becomes a new bottleneck" risk, because for platform work it's usually real.

## Rollout plan
Phases, and the order that makes them safe. State explicitly when (if ever) the old path closes — and what must be true first.

## Success metrics
Before → target numbers, and how you'll measure them. Adoption belongs here.

## Open questions
The things you genuinely haven't decided. Leaving these honest is what makes review useful.
